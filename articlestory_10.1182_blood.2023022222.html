Full narrative

Weeks and Ebert present a concise, influential synthesis of clonal hematopoiesis, framing it as an age-associated expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell clones with important consequences for blood biology, cancer risk, and broader disease mechanisms. Published in Blood in 2023, the article has accumulated 161 citations and sits in the top 1% of papers by field and year, indicating strong academic uptake soon after publication.

The citation landscape suggests that the article has been especially useful as a foundation for work in hematologic malignancy and related translational areas. Citing papers cluster around acute myeloid leukemia research, myeloproliferative neoplasms, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and cancer genomics, which is consistent with the article’s focus on clonal hematopoiesis as both a premalignant state and a biologically informative model of somatic evolution in the hematopoietic system.

Downstream reuse is broad and methodologically diverse: the article is reused by articles, preprints, reviews, book chapters, and an editorial, showing that it functions not only as a research reference but also as a conceptual source for synthesis and interpretation. Recent citing papers include studies on clonal dynamics after stem cell transplantation and reviews of clonal hematopoiesis in immune, neurological, and age-related disease contexts, suggesting that the article has helped shape how the field connects clonal hematopoiesis to clinical and mechanistic questions.

Public-facing attention is modest but present. The work is mentioned on Wikipedia pages for Leukemia and Clonal hematopoiesis, and it has appeared in a Reddit Scholar post and a Hypothesis annotation attached to a related preprint review. Scite also records mostly mentioning citations, alongside a small number of supporting and contradicting statements, which points to an active but still developing interpretive literature rather than a settled consensus.

Overall, the record shows a highly visible and widely reused article that has become part of the core scholarly conversation about clonal hematopoiesis. Its influence is strongest within hematology, oncology, and stem cell biology, where it is being used to organize evidence, motivate new studies, and connect somatic clonality to aging and disease.
